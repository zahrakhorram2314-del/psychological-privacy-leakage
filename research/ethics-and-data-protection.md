# Ethics and Data Protection

## Core principle
Study privacy behavior without collecting private disclosures. Participants must never need to reveal real personal information.

## Before recruitment
1. Check university/course supervisor requirements and applicable local rules.
2. Obtain required ethics review or approval before recruitment and data collection.
3. Use voluntary adult-only recruitment.
4. Prepare participant information, consent, withdrawal instructions, and debrief.
5. Test that the prototype makes no external network calls and uses no analytics, cookies, or hidden logging.

## Tell participants
- The task concerns choices in a fictional AI interaction.
- All profile details are invented.
- No real personal, family, health, location, school, workplace, or account information should be entered.
- Participation is voluntary and may be stopped at any time.
- The study is exploratory, not a psychological assessment.
- Explain exactly what data are collected, storage/access arrangements, and deletion timing.

## Do not collect
Real names, usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, school/workplace or precise location; family/relationship details; health or mental-health information; passwords, authentication codes, financial details, private chat histories; voice/video; IP addresses or persistent device identifiers unless strictly required and separately justified.

Prefer a static local mock-up recording only anonymous response choices. If collection is unnecessary, use a researcher-only walkthrough and label it accurately.

## Storage and access
If approved, use random study codes, institution-approved storage, minimum necessary data, restricted access, and a pre-defined retention/deletion date. Do not publish row-level data if combinations could identify someone.

## Distress and autonomy
Do not ask participants to recall distressing events. Every item may be skipped. If a participant volunteers sensitive information despite instructions, do not reproduce it in the dataset or paper; follow the approved secure handling/deletion process and consult the supervisor.

## Scope
This is a practical safeguard checklist, not legal advice or formal ethics approval. Requirements vary by institution and jurisdiction.
