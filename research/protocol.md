# Research Protocol

**Title:** Psychological Privacy Leakage: How Conversational AI Tone and Contextual Privacy Cues Shape Disclosure Decisions  
**Version:** 0.1 — proposed protocol; no participant data collected.

## Abstract
Conversational agents use social language that may shape users' disclosure choices. Effects are likely context-dependent rather than inevitable. This project proposes a small 2 × 2 feasibility pilot examining conversational tone (warm/socially expressive vs neutral/task-focused) and a contextual privacy notice (present vs no additional notice) in a fictional chatbot interaction. Participants choose only among invented, non-identifying profile details. Outcomes include choice patterns, perceived warmth, privacy awareness, and notice recall. The pilot is intended to assess feasibility and stimulus clarity, not establish population-level effects.

**Keywords:** conversational AI; self-disclosure; anthropomorphism; privacy-by-design; human–AI interaction; usable privacy.

## Background
A conversational agent is both an apparent social partner and a technical service. Warmth, informality, perceived anonymity, topic sensitivity, and privacy expectations may shape disclosure decisions. A review by Papneja and Yadav (2025) identifies multiple influences on self-disclosure to conversational AI and reports context-dependent findings. Zhang and Zhu (2026) examine actual self-disclosure to anthropomorphic chatbots through a contextual privacy-calculus approach. Ho et al. (2018) study psychological and relational effects of self-disclosure after chatbot conversations. Meng and Liu (2025) investigate information disclosure to AI chatbots in a privacy-calculus context. This project therefore tests a bounded question rather than assuming friendliness necessarily increases disclosure.

## Research question
How do conversational tone and a contextual privacy cue influence disclosure choices and privacy awareness in a hypothetical AI interaction?

## Tentative hypotheses
- **H1:** Warm/socially expressive tone may be associated with a different rate of selecting fictional profile details than neutral/task-focused tone.
- **H2:** A contextual privacy notice may increase privacy awareness and may reduce selection of higher-sensitivity fictional details.
- **H3:** The privacy notice may moderate the association between tone and disclosure choice.

These are hypotheses, not findings. The direction and magnitude of any effect remain empirical questions.

## Design
A 2 × 2 between-participants feasibility pilot: (1) warm tone + notice, (2) warm tone + no additional notice, (3) neutral tone + notice, and (4) neutral tone + no additional notice. The same scenario, fictional cards, response options, and task length are used in all conditions.

## Participants and procedure
A tentative target of 40–60 adults (18+) recruited by convenience sampling, only if permitted. Aim for balanced allocation across conditions. Obtain informed consent, confirm adult eligibility, randomly assign a condition, present the scenario and cards, collect optional choices and brief post-task ratings, then debrief. Estimated duration: 5–8 minutes. Participants may stop at any time without penalty.

If ethics approval or recruitment is not feasible, retain this as a protocol/design case study. A researcher-only walkthrough is not participant evidence.

## Measures
**Primary:** binary selection (share/skip) for each fictional card; a pre-defined sensitivity-weighted selection score.  
**Exploratory secondary items** (1 = strongly disagree; 5 = strongly agree): perceived warmth; awareness that the agent is AI; notice recall; privacy awareness; perceived freedom to skip. These single items are not validated psychological scales.  
**Manipulation checks:** perceived warmth and recall of notice assignment.

## Privacy-by-design intervention
The notice should explain what information is requested, why it is relevant, that sharing is optional, and that real identifying or sensitive information must not be entered. Provide a clear skip option. Avoid guilt, urgency, emotional pressure, exclusivity, or promises of confidentiality.

## Ethics and safety
Use fictional pre-written options only. Do not request names, locations, school/workplace details, contact details, family or health information, passwords, or actual secrets. Do not collect chat logs, audio, IP addresses, or unnecessary identifiers. Prefer a static mock-up with no external API, analytics, or third-party logging. Do not recruit minors. Check applicable institutional, course, and local ethics requirements before recruitment or data collection. This protocol is not ethics approval.

## Limitations
Hypothetical choices are not real-world disclosure. A small convenience sample limits power and generalizability. Self-reported ratings may not reflect behavior. One scenario cannot represent all agents, cultures, languages, or sensitive contexts. The pilot cannot establish long-term effects or prove real-world data leakage.

## Reporting rule
Only add a results section after actual data collection. Report recruitment, sample size, exclusions, condition counts, analysis decisions, and limitations. Never invent or simulate participant findings.
